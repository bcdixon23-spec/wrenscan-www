Bitdefender Home Scanner is gone: free ways to check your home network now

25 November 2026 · 3 min read

Checked on 25 November 2026. We make Wrenscan, one of the options below, so read this with that in mind.

For years, Bitdefender Home Scanner was the answer to "is anything on my home network unsafe?". It was free, it ran on Windows, and it checked the devices on your Wi-Fi (printers, cameras, routers, smart TVs) for known security holes. Plenty of "best free network scanner" lists still recommend it.

It isn't offered any more: Bitdefender's Home Scanner page now takes you to its free antivirus instead. So if you've just gone looking for it, here's what you can use today.

What you actually want from a home network scanner

  • A list of every device, so you can spot the one you don't recognise.
  • A check of each device for the common problems: old file sharing, open remote access, default passwords on printers, an exposed router admin page, weak encryption.
  • Answers you can act on: what the problem is and how to fix it, not a wall of codes.

The free options

Fing (phones, Windows, Mac) is excellent at the first job: a fast, accurate list of every device, with the maker and model. The free version allows up to 3 scans a day. Security checks are limited on the free plan; router vulnerability checks and continuous monitoring are part of Fing Premium. You'll need a Fing account.

Avast Free Antivirus includes a Wi-Fi network check alongside the antivirus. Good if you want antivirus anyway; less useful if you only want the network check.

Nmap is the free tool security professionals use (Bitdefender's scanner was reported to use it under the hood). It's powerful and completely free, but it's a command-line tool: you'll need to know what you're looking at.

OpenVAS / Greenbone Community Edition is a full, free vulnerability scanner. It's deep, but setting it up is a project in itself, and it's built for IT teams.

Wrenscan (Windows 10 and 11) is what we built to fill exactly this gap. It:

  • finds every device on your network, including ones that ignore ordinary scans, and names its maker and type;
  • runs 118 plain-English checks across them (printers answering to the default "public" password, cameras with Telnet open, NAS boxes still offering SMBv1, routers that opened ports to the internet through UPnP, and the security settings of the PC it runs on);
  • ranks what it finds by what attackers are actually using right now, so the urgent things come first;
  • explains every problem and how to fix it, and can fix many problems on your PC with one click, with an undo.

It's free for up to 15 devices per scan and 15 scans a month, with no account. Your results stay on your PC. It never tries passwords or runs exploits against your devices.

Which should you use?

  • Just want to see what's on your Wi-Fi? Fing.
  • Already want antivirus? Avast Free covers the basics.
  • Comfortable with the command line? Nmap.
  • Want each device checked and the fixes explained, the way Home Scanner did it? Wrenscan.
