Wrenscan vs Nessus Essentials: a free vulnerability scanner for your home or office

25 November 2026 · 4 min read

Facts checked on Tenable's website on 25 November 2026. We make Wrenscan, so read this with that in mind; we've tried to be fair, including about where Nessus is the better tool.

Tenable's Nessus is one of the most widely used vulnerability scanners in the world, and security professionals trust it for good reason. Its free edition, Nessus Essentials, has long been the first thing people recommend for scanning a home network. Tenable has since changed the free edition, so here's how it compares with Wrenscan today.

What changed with Nessus Essentials

According to Tenable's product page, Nessus Essentials is now:

  • Free for 5 IP addresses, on a 30-day licence that you register for. Scan data isn't kept when the licence expires.
  • For non-commercial use only: hobbyists, students and evaluation. Tenable says commercial use needs a paid Nessus Professional licence.
  • Without real-time plugin updates (a 30-day delay), reporting or data export, or technical support.

The paid options are Nessus Essentials Plus at $199 a year (20 IPs), Nessus Professional at $4,790 a year (unlimited IPs) and Nessus Expert at $6,790 a year.

Five addresses doesn't go far in a modern home, where a router, two laptops, a few phones, a TV, a printer and some smart plugs already make a dozen.

Where Nessus is the better choice

  • You need depth. Nessus has a far larger library of vulnerability tests than Wrenscan, covering thousands of products and versions. For a security professional or a company with compliance requirements, the paid Nessus editions are the standard for good reason.
  • You're learning security. Nessus is an industry tool, and the free edition is a good way for students and hobbyists to learn it.
  • You scan Linux servers and applications in depth. Nessus's credentialed scanning and plugin coverage go well beyond Wrenscan's.

Where Wrenscan is the better choice

  • You have more than five devices. Wrenscan's free version covers up to 15 devices per scan, 15 scans a month, with no expiry.
  • It's for your business. Wrenscan's free version can be used at work; Nessus Essentials can't.
  • You want answers, not a report. Wrenscan ranks what it finds by what attackers are actually exploiting (CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list and EPSS scores), explains each item in plain English, and on the PC it runs on can fix many of them for you, check the fix worked, and undo it.
  • You want to see every device. Wrenscan finds what's on the network, including devices that ignore pings, and tells you what each one is, instead of starting from a list of addresses.

The short version

If you're a security professional, or you need to prove compliance, use Nessus (the paid editions). If you want to find out what's wrong with your home or small-office network and fix it this evening, that's what Wrenscan is for.
